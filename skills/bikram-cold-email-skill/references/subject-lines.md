# Subject Line Optimization

Choose a subject that gives this audience a concrete, honest reason to read the actual email. Specificity and subject/body fit matter more than minimum length or an internal-looking style. Permission-based promotions and explicitly supplied subjects follow [campaign-modes.md](campaign-modes.md); do not force them into cold-email conventions.

## Choose the angle, then the length

- Write the body first, then compare a few different reasons this recipient would read: a relevant goal/opportunity, a useful answer to a workflow question, or a verified trigger. Pick the strongest body-backed hook, not merely the clearest description of your service. Return one unless variants are requested; keep deliberation private.
- Use enough words to distinguish this offer from a category label. Short can work when specific; longer can work when each added word clarifies relevance. No mandatory word count, capitalization style or curiosity gap.
- Front-load the useful topic for mobile previews. Cut filler and unsupported qualifiers, not the detail that makes the subject meaningful.
- "Too short" feedback calls for useful specificity, not an expanded slogan. Preserve an explicitly requested safe subject, length or style. A subject-only rewrite does not change the body or permanently update brand preferences. If a body is supplied to keep unchanged, return the new subject with that exact body (including punctuation and line breaks), unless the operator explicitly requests output of the subject alone.


Generate subjects from this brief, not stock examples in this reference. Center the buyer's primary goal and make the body pay it off; do not select a supporting feature merely because it offers a clever question. Avoid hiring/cost-saving angles without that being the selected purchase reason, loaded either/or diagnostics, and long questions packed with workflow jargon. A concrete possible benefit can be relevant without asserting a known pain. Do not manufacture intrigue or imply audit findings before doing the work. No hook proves higher opens.

## Honest one-to-one tone

Use natural language suited to the brand. An internal-note style is optional, never a disguise: no fake Re:/Fwd:, invented prior conversation or implied employment relationship. An accurate service-led subject is allowed without turning it into a slogan. The body must immediately deliver what the subject suggests.

## Capitalization

Use natural sentence case or the requested brand style, preserving proper names. Lowercase is optional, not an optimization rule. Avoid decorative capitalization and ALL CAPS unless an explicitly requested format justifies them.

## Personalization: context over name

Personalize the topic, not just the salutation. A real hiring initiative, operational pain, or current project gives the recipient a reason to open; their first name alone does not.
- Use pain points, trigger events, or industry challenges tied directly to your offer.
- Use verified contextual fields such as `[PAIN_POINT]`, `[COMPETITOR]`, or `[COMMON_GROUND]` only when they exist on the selected list; otherwise use literal segment-level copy. Avoid `[FNAME]` in B2B cold subjects by default.

## Questions: only when highly specific

Use a question when it conveys a specific, relevant reason to read. Generic "Quick question?" supplies no context. A question, statement or fragment can work; choose by meaning and body payoff, not a preferred grammatical shape.

## What to Avoid

| Anti-pattern | Risk |
|--------------|------|
| Product slogans and generic sales promises | Look like mass promotion before the recipient sees the value |
| Generic urgency ("urgent," "ASAP") | Feels manufactured; use a real deadline only when relevant |
| Excessive punctuation or ALL CAPS | Looks like a broadcast, not a personal note |
| Unsupported numbers or guarantees | Creates a promise the body cannot substantiate |
| Decorative emojis or name-only personalization | Often signals automation in B2B cold outreach |
| Fake Re:/Fwd: or invented prior conversation | Falsely claims a relationship or thread |

Honest approved offer-specific subjects remain appropriate for permission-based promotions; see [campaign-modes.md](campaign-modes.md).

## C-Suite Subject Lines

For executives, lead with a relevant business goal or workflow the email actually addresses. Keep enough context to make the reason to read clear; do not force a bare topic such as "forecast gaps" when a more specific subject is warranted. Never assert an unverified initiative or pain.

Use a verified `[COMPANY_INITIATIVE]` field or literal project reference when available. Do not use "thank you" or "an update" to imply prior contact that did not occur.

## Test the Hook, Not Just the Wording

When variants are requested, test different reasons to open: problem vs initiative vs curiosity. Keep the audience, body, and timing comparable so you can interpret the difference. Supply usable subjects first, then a short explanation of each angle; do not replace requested copy with a tracking or compliance lecture.

Use approved tracking settings and actual available metrics; do not change them for a report. Opens may include privacy prefetches and clicks may include scanners. Compare subjects using comparable cohorts and observation windows; identify unique versus total events and rate denominators. Include positive replies or conversions only when verified, and never promise an open-rate lift.
