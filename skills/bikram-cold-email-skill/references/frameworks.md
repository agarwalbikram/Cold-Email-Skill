# Cold Email Copywriting Frameworks

Use these as thinking structures, not text to copy. Choose for the selected brand, audience, offer, evidence and campaign mode; no framework is the default or guaranteed to perform. Use fewer steps or write freeform when that makes the email clearer.

## Evidence and mode first

- Examples are fictional teaching scenarios, not client facts. Each scenario names the facts it assumes; use equivalent facts only when supplied or verified for the selected brand. Never copy its observation, capability or customer story into another client's email as evidence.
- No customer proof? Use an approved capability, useful sample or relevant question. Omit the proof step rather than inventing customers, numbers, endorsements or results. Treat unverified recipient pain as a possibility, not a diagnosis.
- A verified trigger must connect to the offer. Without one, use honest segment-level relevance instead of flattery or invented research. Never infer sensitive personal circumstances.
- For B2B cold first touches, prefer one low-friction reply CTA. Permission-based promotions may use one approved offer-link CTA and accurate offer-led subjects; preserve affiliate identity, disclosures and terms under [campaign-modes.md](campaign-modes.md).
- The quoted examples show body cores, not complete send-ready messages. Add the selected sender's required signature, postal address, disclosures and opt-out/unsubscribe content. No recipient tags or editorial placeholders are needed in these examples; actual MailWizz tags still require validation.

## PAS — Problem, Agitate, Solution

**Structure:** Relevant problem → Practical consequence → Capability + small ask.
**Use when:** The audience has a supported problem. Keep the consequence proportionate; do not manufacture fear.

Scenario: the brief targets sales managers who compile weekly CRM reports; the product's export capability is confirmed. No customer results are available.
> Does compiling the weekly CRM report still involve spreadsheets? Rebuilding the same report leaves less time to review it with the team. Our tool exports a weekly summary from your CRM. Would a sample report help?

## BAB — Before, After, Bridge

**Structure:** Current workflow → Concrete alternative → How the offer supports it.
**Use when:** A supported workflow change is easier to explain than a feature list. Describe the alternative without promising results.

Scenario: an agency offer includes reusable briefs and reviewer checklists for teams currently coordinating approvals by email.
> If content approvals are scattered across email threads, a shared reviewer checklist gives everyone the same handoff. We build those checklists around your current approval process. Want to see an example?

## QVC — Question, Value, CTA

**Structure:** Specific question → Relevant capability → Easy next step.
**Use when:** A brief question can establish fit without asserting the recipient has a problem.

Scenario: a research tool can assemble account briefs from public company information.
> How does your team prepare account briefs before outreach? Our tool brings public company information into one brief for a rep to review. Would a sample be useful?

## AIDA — Attention, Interest, Desire, Action

**Structure:** Relevant hook → Useful capability → Tangible benefit → One CTA.
**Use when:** The offer needs a little explanation. Attention does not require a sensational statistic.

Scenario: a verified job posting mentions manual lead assignment; the product supports routing rules and an operator-approved routing example exists.
> Your sales-ops posting mentions manual lead assignment. Our routing tool assigns incoming leads using rules your team sets, so ownership is visible in one place. Want to see an example routing setup?

## PPP — Praise, Picture, Push

**Structure:** Specific acknowledgment → Relevant possibility → Gentle invitation.
**Use when:** A real observation merits acknowledgment and connects to the offer. Skip praise if it is only decoration.

Scenario: a verified talk discusses practice calls during onboarding; the offer provides role-specific practice scenarios.
> Your onboarding talk focused on giving new reps more practice before live calls. We build role-specific practice scenarios for that stage. Would an example fit what you're planning?

## Star-Story-Solution

**Structure:** Customer situation → Work performed → Documented change → Relevant ask.
**Use when:** An approved customer story exists, with permission to use its details. Without one, choose a capability-led structure instead.

Scenario: an approved anonymized case documents a team consolidating its intake process into a shared form; no time-saving or revenue result is established.
> A studio we worked with had project requests arriving in several inboxes. We helped them introduce one intake form with a named owner for each request. Want to see the form's structure?

## SCQ — Situation, Complication, Question

**Structure:** Verified situation → Possible implication → Genuine question.
**Use when:** You know the situation but not how the recipient handles it. Leave room for their answer.

Scenario: current job listings confirm hiring in two locations; no onboarding problem has been verified.
> You're hiring support reps in two locations. That can add another handoff to onboarding. How are you keeping training consistent across the teams?

## ACCA — Awareness, Comprehension, Conviction, Action

**Structure:** Relevant issue → Clear mechanism → Available evidence → Small ask.
**Use when:** An analytical audience needs to understand how the offer works. Evidence can be an inspectable sample, not a performance claim.

Scenario: a reporting service can show report logic, and a permission-cleared example is available.
> A dashboard total is easier to review when you can trace it back to the calculation. Our reporting service includes the logic behind each metric. Would you like an example with the calculation shown?

## 3C's — Compliment, Case Study, CTA

**Structure:** Specific acknowledgment → Relevant approved case → Easy ask.
**Use when:** Both a meaningful observation and a relevant customer case exist. Without either, use a simpler structure; don't invent admiration or proof.

Scenario: a verified launch introduced self-serve signup; an approved anonymized case documents work on a comparable onboarding flow, without measured conversion results.
> Your new self-serve signup lets teams try the product before talking to sales. We helped another software team map the handoffs in a similar onboarding flow. Would an outline of that work be useful?

## Mouse Trap — Observation + Value Question

**Structure:** Relevant observation → One concrete value question.
**Use when:** A verified signal and a small useful offer fit in one or two sentences.

Scenario: hiring is verified and the sender has an approved rep-onboarding checklist.
> You're hiring account executives. Would a checklist for their first week of account research be useful?

## Justin Michael Method — Trigger, Solution Hint, Binary CTA

**Structure:** Relevant signal → Brief capability → Easy yes/no question.
**Use when:** Brevity suits the audience. Precision matters more than being provocative.

Scenario: a verified interview identifies approval delays; the sender has a relevant handoff template.
> In your interview, you mentioned waiting on approvals between project stages. We build handoff templates that name the next reviewer. Want to see one?

## Vanilla Ice Cream — Observation, Insight, Credibility, Solution, Conversation

**Structure:** Connected observation → Possible issue → Relevant evidence/capability → Small ask.
**Use when:** The elements form a natural short argument. Combine or omit steps rather than padding the email to fit all five.

Scenario: a verified pricing page has several plans; the sender offers moderated plan-selection tests and has a permission-cleared example.
> Your pricing page has separate plans for individuals and teams. We run moderated tests of how buyers choose between plans. Would an example test outline help with your next page review?

## PASTOR — Problem, Amplify, Story, Testimony, Offer, Response

**Structure:** Relevant need → Practical consequence → Supported story/evidence if available → Clear offer → One response.
**Use when:** A permission-based promotion or requested sequence needs a fuller explanation. Do not stretch a cold first touch into a sales letter or require every step. Each sequence email adds useful information rather than escalating pressure.

Scenario: subscribers explicitly opted into this sender's project-management promotions; a third-party workbook is approved, its seller-confirmed price is $29 once, and an approved affiliate destination exists. No testimonial is available, so story/testimony are omitted. Attach the actual approved URL to the CTA only when composing the campaign.
> If project handoffs are getting lost in email, this workbook offers a checklist for assigning owners and reviewers. It costs $29 as a one-time purchase. I may earn a commission if you buy through my link. View the workbook.

Keep price, renewal terms, genuine deadlines and disclosures accurate; never turn a third-party offer into "our product." Use [copy-quality.md](copy-quality.md) for the final wording review. These structures do not change campaign approval, tracking choices or scheduling rules.
